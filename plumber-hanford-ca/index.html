When plumbing trouble in Hanford cannot wait

An emergency plumber in Hanford, CA should answer when a pipe has burst, a toilet will not stop overflowing, a sewer line is backing up into a tub or a water heater is leaking onto a floor. These are 24/7 problems, and we treat them that way.

Call the emergency line, describe what you see and follow the first steps while the plumber is on the way. Travel time from Corcoran is an estimate that depends on roads and traffic, so the dispatcher will give you an arrival window on the call and update you if it changes.

Shut off the water

Turn the main valve clockwise. In Hanford it is often near the meter at the front of the lot.

Stop the backup

Do not flush or run water if sewage is rising in a tub or floor drain.

Power down the heater

Turn off power or gas to a leaking water heater, and shut its cold-water valve.

Protect the area

Older homes near downtown and newer homes on the edges

The 2010 census counted 18,493 housing units in Hanford, and about 58 percent were owner-occupied. Streets near the historic downtown, around Courthouse Square and the old railroad corridor, include homes built decades ago. Older homes may still have galvanized steel, cast iron drains or early copper, all of which can corrode, crack or clog with age.

Newer subdivisions on the edges of town tend to have PEX or copper supply lines and PVC drains. Those materials last longer, but poor fittings, high pressure or a missing expansion tank can still cause trouble. A plumber who knows both eras can tell quickly which problem you have.

Many homes in the Central Valley sit on concrete slabs, so a leak under the floor can go unseen for a long time. If your water bill rises with no change in habits, call before a wet spot shows up.

How Hanford's groundwater system matters for your plumbing

The City of Hanford's water system is groundwater-based. The city's water system page says no surface water is used and that water is pumped from 13 deep wells, with well depths between 600 and 1,700 feet. The system serves about 15,900 connections through 203 miles of mains.

Groundwater carries dissolved minerals, and the amount depends on the wells and the blend. The city publishes a consumer confidence report each year, and that report is the right place to look for hardness and other tested results. We do not quote a hardness figure for Hanford here, because the number you need is the one in the latest report.

The city also runs a cross-connection control program with about 1,700 backflow prevention assemblies tested once a year. If you own a business with a backflow device, you can ask the city's Water Division what testing it requires.

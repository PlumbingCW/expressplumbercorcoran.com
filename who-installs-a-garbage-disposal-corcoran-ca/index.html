Who Installs a Garbage Disposal for Corcoran, CA Homes?

A plumber usually handles a disposal swap, and the electrical side is often simple. Here is who does what.

Written by the Corcoran Express Plumbing team · Published 2026

If you want a garbage disposal under your kitchen sink, the first question is who to call. In most Corcoran, CA homes, a plumber does the whole job when the power outlet is already under the sink. An electrician may be needed if there is no outlet or switch.

This guide explains the roles, the steps of a typical installation, what the job needs and when it is reasonable to do it yourself. It also covers what to check before you buy a unit.

Plumber, electrician or you

A plumber connects the disposal to the sink drain, the dishwasher hose and the trap. If a switch and outlet already exist, that is all the work needed. If the sink has never had a disposal, an electrician may need to add a switch and a receptacle under the sink.

Handy owners do replace a worn disposal. The mounting hardware is standardized for many brands, so a like-for-like swap is simple. A first-time installation is more work because the sink drain, the baffle and the wiring all change.

What a typical installation involves

The plumber removes the old unit or the sink strainer, mounts the new flange, hangs the disposal on the mounting ring and connects the discharge pipe to the trap. The dishwasher drain hose connects to a port on the side after the knockout plug is removed.

Then the unit is wired or plugged in, the connections are leak-tested and the disposal is run with water. The job usually takes about an hour for a swap and longer for a new install.

What to have ready

  • The brand and model of any existing unit.
  • Clear space under the sink.
  • A working outlet and switch, or plan for an electrician.
  • Knowledge of whether you have a dishwasher hookup.

Choosing a unit

Horsepower, noise level and feed type are the main choices. A continuous-feed model starts with a wall switch. A batch-feed model runs only when the stopper is turned. Higher horsepower grinds tougher waste but costs more.

Think about your habits. A household that cooks often may want a stronger motor, while a small kitchen may prefer a compact model that leaves cabinet space. If your drain is old or slow, address that first. See garbage disposal repair and installation for what the plumber checks.

Septic and drain considerations

A disposal sends food waste into the drain. In homes on septic systems, that adds solids and may fill the tank faster. Check your septic maker's guidance before you add one. In homes on city sewer, the main concern is the condition of the drain line.

Old cast iron or clogged drains can back up when a disposal is added. If your kitchen sink already drains slowly, a drain cleaning visit can clear the line before the new unit goes in.

What not to put down a disposal

Grease, coffee grounds in large amounts, bones, fibrous vegetable scraps, pasta and rice can clog a disposal or the drain. Run cold water before, during and after use. Fats and oils harden in the pipe and build up over time.

Never put your hand in a disposal, even when it is off. If it jams, turn off the power at the switch and the breaker, then use a hex key at the bottom of the unit to free it.

How to compare installers

Ask whether the installer is a licensed plumber, whether the price includes the disposal or just the labor and what happens if a leak shows up later. A written price with the model number avoids surprises.

Ask how long the installer expects the job to take and whether the old unit will be hauled away. Some installers charge extra for disposal of the old unit, and some do not.

Safety and noise

A disposal runs on a strong motor and can be loud. Look for models with sound insulation if your kitchen is open to the living room. Always turn off power at the breaker before any work, since a switch alone does not remove all risk.

Keep children and pets away from the sink during installation, and teach everyone in the house not to put a hand in the unit.

Alternatives to a disposal

Not every home needs one. A compost pail or a green waste bin can handle food scraps with no plumbing. In homes with old or slow drains, skipping the disposal may avoid clogs.

If you do install one, a regular schedule of cold water and mild cleaners helps. Citrus peels and ice cubes are common ways to freshen the unit, but they do not replace good habits.

Care after installation

Run cold water for about fifteen seconds after each use so scraps clear the trap arm. Grind small amounts at a time instead of a full sink of scraps, and avoid chemical drain openers, which can damage seals.

If the disposal hums but does not spin, it is probably jammed. Turn off power first and free it with the hex key at the bottom, then press the reset button on the unit.

Emergency toilet repair in Corcoran, CA is for the overflowing bowl, the leak at the base, the toilet that will not stop running and the cracked tank that sends water across the floor. The 24/7 line is open at night and on weekends, and a plumber can be sent the same day when schedules allow.

Toilet repair includes replacing the fill valve and flapper, resetting or replacing the wax ring, repairing a damaged flange, clearing a clog and installing a new toilet when repair is not worthwhile.

When a toilet problem cannot wait

A toilet that overflows, leaks at the floor or floods from a cracked tank is an emergency. Water on a bathroom floor spreads to subfloors and walls fast.

Close the shutoff valve behind the toilet and stop flushing. If water is still flowing, shut the main.

Shutoff valve

Turn the oval handle at the wall clockwise until it stops.

Lift the tank lid

Raise the float or flapper chain to stop the fill if the valve is stuck.

Mop and dry

Use towels and a fan to dry the floor and protect the subfloor.

Do not keep flushing

More flushing adds water to a blocked trap.

Running toilets and why they waste water

A toilet that hisses or refills every few minutes is usually leaking from the tank to the bowl through a worn flapper. A failing fill valve can also keep water flowing into the overflow tube.

Both parts are inexpensive, but a running toilet can waste a lot of water in a month. If you pay for water by the gallon, the bill rises. A quick repair usually pays for itself.

Leaks at the base: wax rings and flanges

A toilet sits on a wax ring that seals the bowl to the drain flange. If water appears around the base, the ring may have failed, the bolts may be loose or the flange may be cracked or set too low.

A plumber pulls the toilet, checks the flange, replaces the ring and resets the bowl. If the flange is broken, a repair ring or replacement is needed. A subfloor that has softened from long leaking may need repair first.

What to check before you call

  • Water pooling at the base after flushing.
  • A toilet that rocks on the floor.
  • A musty smell in the room.
  • Soft or spongy flooring near the toilet.

What to do before the plumber arrives

  • Close the stop valve under the fixture or behind the toilet.
  • Turn off power to a garbage disposal before any work.
  • Never put a hand into a disposal, even when it is off.
  • Clear the area under the sink so the plumber can reach the pipes.
  • Note when the problem started and what changed.

Clogs, slow flushes and when it is not the toilet

A single clogged toilet can often be cleared with a plunger or a toilet auger. If every fixture in the house drains slowly, the clog is in the line and the visit should be treated as a drain cleaning call.

Repeated clogs with a normal flush may point to a partial blockage in the trap or a vent issue. The plumber will check the bowl, the trap and the line before proposing a fix.

Repair or replace the toilet

An older toilet that needs a new flange, a new tank hardware set and has stains or cracks may be cheaper to replace than to repair. A new water-efficient model also uses less water per flush.

If you want to upgrade, the plumber can measure the rough-in distance and recommend a model that fits. Installation is covered on the plumbing installation page for new bathrooms and remodels.

Code and licensing for fixture and piping work

Fixture and piping work follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). It sets rules for traps, vents, fixture clearances and approved materials, and it is the standard a plumbing inspector checks against.

Minor repairs often need no permit, while new fixtures, relocated drains and whole-house piping usually do. Ask the City of Corcoran building department about your project.

How to Repair a Leaky Bathtub Faucet in Corcoran, CA

A dripping tub faucet wastes water and stains the tub. Most of the time a worn part is the cause, and the fix is simple.

Written by the Corcoran Express Plumbing team · Published 2026

A tub faucet that drips after you shut it off is usually leaking through a worn washer, seat or cartridge inside the faucet. The drip may seem small, but a faucet that drips ten times a minute can waste hundreds of gallons in a year. A stain in the tub is the first sign most Corcoran homeowners notice.

This guide covers the common types of tub faucets, the parts that wear out, a basic repair sequence and the signs that call for a plumber. Shutting off the water before you start is the most important step.

Identify your faucet type first

Tub faucets come in four main types. A compression faucet has two handles and uses rubber washers. A cartridge faucet has one or two handles and a replaceable cartridge. A ball faucet has one handle and a rotating ball. A ceramic disk faucet has one handle and a sealed disk cylinder.

Each type has different parts and a different repair. Look for a brand name on the handle or a model number inside the faucet after you remove the handle. A photo helps a plumbing supply counter match the part.

What wears out and why

Rubber washers harden and crack. Seats wear a groove. Cartridges lose their seals and let water slip past. Over time, mineral scale and sediment can also scratch the seals and hold the valve slightly open.

Use is the main factor. A faucet used daily will wear faster than a guest bath faucet. The age of the house matters too. Older faucets may use parts that are no longer made, and replacing the whole valve may be the sensible answer.

Tools and parts to gather

  • Screwdriver set and an adjustable wrench.
  • Replacement cartridge or washer set matched to your model.
  • Plumber's grease and a rag.
  • A bucket and towels to catch water.

A basic repair sequence

Shut off the water at the main or at the tub's stops if you have access panels. Open the faucet to drain the pipes. Remove the handle, trim plate and the stem or cartridge, taking photos at each step.

Replace the cartridge or the washer and seat, add a little plumber's grease to the seals and reassemble. Turn the water back on slowly and check for drips. If the valve body is corroded or the cartridge will not come out, stop and call a plumber.

When the problem is the shower side or the valve

If the tub spout drips only when the shower is running, the diverter may be failing. If water seeps behind the wall or into the ceiling below, the valve or a supply connection may be leaking. Those are not simple cartridge jobs.

Wall leaks need a plumber who can open the wall at the valve and test the connections. A faucet repair visit can handle both the faucet and the valve. If you see wetness elsewhere, a leak detection check finds the source.

Replace the faucet or repair it?

If the faucet is newer and the part is available, repair is the cheaper path. If the valve body is corroded, the finish is worn or the parts are discontinued, replacement is better. New valves also give more reliable temperature control.

Plan for access. Replacing a tub valve may mean opening the wall behind the tub, sometimes through an access panel from a closet or hall. A plumber can explain what is needed and give a clear price before work starts.

How to stop the drip while you wait

If the faucet will not shut off, close the stop valves if the tub has them, or shut off the main water valve. Place a towel or a bucket under the spout so the water does not run across the floor. A drip that is slow can wait for a scheduled visit, but a stream cannot.

Avoid overtightening the handle to stop the drip. Forcing it can strip the stem or crack the seat and turn a simple cartridge swap into a valve replacement.

Why a drip wastes more than you expect

A faucet that drips once a second loses a few gallons a day, and that adds up to thousands of gallons over a year. Hot water drips cost more because you also pay to heat the water that runs away.

Fixing a drip early is almost always cheaper than waiting. A worn cartridge is a small part, while a seat that has been cut by a long leak may need a new valve.

Keeping the tub area dry and sound

Check the caulk where the tub meets the wall, and look for loose tiles around the faucet. Water that gets behind the wall can rot the framing and grow mold. If the tile is soft or the wall sounds hollow, ask a plumber to look before you re-caulk.

Corcoran, CA: soft water and tub faucet wear

The City of Corcoran's 2021 water quality report shows hardness ranging from 4.3 to 140 ppm in samples taken in 2019 and 2020, with an average of 52 ppm. That mix means most taps get soft water, but some samples ran harder. A cartridge that sticks may be scaled in one home and clean in the next.

If you pull a cartridge and see white crust, a vinegar soak can loosen it. If the seals are cracked, the part is simply old.

A faucet that will not shut off or a valve that is leaking in the wall needs help quickly. We repair or replace it the same day when possible.

Emergency faucet repair in Corcoran, CA covers a faucet that will not shut off, a handle that has broken, a supply line spraying under a sink or a shower valve that keeps running. The 24/7 line is open at night and on weekends, and a plumber can be sent the same day when schedules allow.

Faucet repair means replacing cartridges, washers and seals, tightening or replacing supply lines and shutoff valves, or installing a new faucet when repair is not worthwhile. Many faucet problems start with a worn part, not a failed pipe.

A faucet that will not shut off

A faucet that will not close, or a supply line that is leaking under a sink, is an emergency when water is flowing across a floor. The first step is to close the nearest shutoff.

Look under the sink for the two small oval or round valves and turn them clockwise. If they are stuck or leaking, shut the main.

Drips, leaks and stiff handles

Most drips come from worn washers, seals or cartridges inside the faucet. A cartridge faucet needs a new cartridge, while an older compression faucet needs a new washer and seat. Corroded parts may need a full faucet replacement.

A handle that is stiff or that will not turn all the way may have mineral or corrosion buildup. In Corcoran, where the city's water averaged 52 ppm hardness in the 2021 report, scale is usually lighter than in hard-water areas, so age and wear are the more common causes.

Shower valves and tub spouts

A shower that drips from the head after you shut it off usually has a worn cartridge or seat in the valve. A tub spout that leaks at the wall may need a new seal or a diverter. Both repairs need access to the valve and sometimes to the wall behind it.

If the valve is old and parts are no longer made, a replacement may be the more reliable option. The plumber will explain the access needed and where the cut goes.

Signs the valve needs more than a cartridge

  • Water seeps behind the wall or through the ceiling below.
  • The handle turns but the temperature does not change.
  • Parts are corroded or cannot be removed.
  • The same valve has been repaired more than once.

What to do before the plumber arrives

  • Close the stop valve under the fixture or behind the toilet.
  • Turn off power to a garbage disposal before any work.
  • Never put a hand into a disposal, even when it is off.
  • Clear the area under the sink so the plumber can reach the pipes.
  • Note when the problem started and what changed.

Shutoff valves and supply lines

Angle stops under sinks and toilets are small valves that wear out over years. If a valve leaks or will not close, replacing it makes later repairs easier. Braided supply lines can burst, so older lines should be replaced when you update a faucet.

Burst supply lines are a common cause of kitchen and bath floods. If you have lines that are more than ten years old, a quick replacement is inexpensive compared with the damage of a burst.

Outdoor spigots and hose bibs

A hose bib that drips at the handle or sprays behind the handle needs a new washer or packing. Valley winters have occasional frosts, and a hose left connected can split a spigot. A split spigot may leak inside the wall.

If the leak is inside, a leak detection visit can find it before damage spreads. If the line itself has failed, the repair may fall under burst pipe repair.

Code and licensing for fixture and piping work

Fixture and piping work follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). It sets rules for traps, vents, fixture clearances and approved materials, and it is the standard a plumbing inspector checks against.

Minor repairs often need no permit, while new fixtures, relocated drains and whole-house piping usually do. Ask the City of Corcoran building department about your project.

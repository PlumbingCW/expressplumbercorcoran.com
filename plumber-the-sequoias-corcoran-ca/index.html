When plumbing trouble in The Sequoias cannot wait

A leaking supply line, an overflowing toilet or a water heater dripping onto the garage floor needs attention right away, even in a home that is only a few years old. Water damage does not care how new the house is.

Stop the water first, then call. Check your paperwork afterward to see whether the builder or an appliance warranty covers the repair.

Shut off the main valve

Most homes have one near the meter or on an outside wall. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

Turn off the water heater

Switch off power or gas and close the cold-water valve if the tank is leaking.

Stop using the toilet or sink

If a drain is backing up, do not run more water into it.

Take photos

Pictures of the damage help with warranty and insurance claims.

Check your warranty before you pay for a repair

Homes sold new usually come with some form of builder warranty, and appliances have their own. Terms differ, and we have not seen the terms for homes in this community. Read your closing documents, and call the builder first if a problem appears soon after you move in.

Warranty coverage often ends after a set time, and some repairs are not covered at all. If a problem is urgent, such as flooding, stop the water and call a plumber. Keep your photos and receipts so you can ask for reimbursement later if the warranty applies.

Water quality and your fixtures

Corcoran's city water report publishes test results each year, including hardness. Read it before you buy a softener or filter, because the right equipment depends on what is actually in your water. A plumber can also check the water pressure at your home, since high pressure can wear fixtures and a water heater early.

If you are not sure whether a spot on a faucet is a leak or just mineral buildup, a quick visit can sort it out. Many fixture problems are simple repairs, and replacing a cartridge costs far less than replacing a whole faucet.

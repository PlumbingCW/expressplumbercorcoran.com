Water Softener and Filtration in Corcoran, CA

Corcoran's city water is soft, so a softener is rarely the first fix. Test your tap, then choose a filter that matches the problem.

☎ Call (661) 338-1180

Water softener and filtration service in Corcoran, CA starts with an honest fact. The City of Corcoran's 2021 water quality report lists average hardness of 52 ppm, which is soft water, so many homes here do not need a softener at all. A sound plan begins with a tap test and the city's own report.

Filtration is different. A filter can improve taste, remove sediment or reduce chlorine odor, and some homes want reverse osmosis at the kitchen sink. We install and service these systems, and we tell you when a system will not solve the problem you describe.

What Corcoran's water report shows

The City of Corcoran uses groundwater only, drawn from nine city wells in the 2021 report. Average hardness is 52 ppm as calcium carbonate, with a range of 4.3 to 140 ppm in 2019 and 2020 samples. Average total dissolved solids was 240 ppm, well under the 1,000 ppm secondary standard. Average nitrate was 4.7 ppm against a limit of 10.

These numbers mean the water is soft to moderately soft, so scale is a light issue. They also mean that a softener is likely unnecessary unless your own tap test shows something different. Read the current report on the city's website and test your own tap if you are unsure.

When a softener makes sense and when it does not

A softener swaps calcium and magnesium for sodium to reduce scale. It helps where hardness is above roughly 120 ppm. In water that averages 52 ppm, a softener adds maintenance, salt and wastewater for little gain.

If you have a private well or live outside the city system, your water may differ, and a lab test is the right start. Some homes also have an old softener from a past owner. We can inspect it, service it or remove it.

Filtration options that match common problems

A sediment filter protects fixtures from sand and grit. A carbon filter reduces chlorine taste and odor. A reverse osmosis system at the kitchen sink reduces dissolved solids and many contaminants for drinking and cooking.

A whole-house filter treats every tap, which helps with sediment and chlorine but needs a filter change on a schedule. For a single drinking tap, a small under-sink system is usually the better fit. Either option can be paired with a water heater check if you see discolored hot water.

Service, filters and honest limits

Filter systems need new cartridges and an annual check. We can set up a maintenance plan and note the dates so you do not forget. We do not claim that a filter removes everything, because each filter type targets specific contaminants.

If your concern is a specific contaminant, ask a certified lab for a test and choose a filter certified for that contaminant.

Code and licensing for treatment equipment

Water treatment equipment is installed under the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). That includes backflow protection on the supply and an air gap on any drain line from a softener or reverse osmosis unit.

Equipment claims should match a third-party certification. Ask which contaminants a unit is certified to reduce, and check the city's latest water quality report before you buy.

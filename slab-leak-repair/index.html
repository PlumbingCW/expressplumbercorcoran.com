Emergency Slab Leak Repair in Corcoran, CA

Warm floors, water sounds and a bill that keeps climbing can point to a leak under the slab. We find it and explain the repair options.

Emergency slab leak repair in Corcoran, CA is the response to water escaping from pipes under a concrete foundation. A slab leak can show as a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water when no tap is open or a meter that keeps turning. The 24/7 line is open now, and a plumber can start the diagnosis the same day when schedules allow.

A slab leak is a leak in a water line, usually hot or cold supply, that runs under or through the concrete foundation. Because the pipe is buried, finding it takes electronic listening equipment, and repair means either breaking the slab at the leak, rerouting the line through the attic or walls or replacing the section.

Slab leak signs that cannot wait

A leak under the slab can soften soil, lift flooring and run up the water bill every hour. Water that reaches a wall socket or a baseboard is a safety issue. If you see water at the floor with no source, shut the main water valve.

Do not ignore warm spots on the floor, which can point to a hot-water line leak. Call early before the leak undermines the slab or wets the structure.

How slab leaks are found

The plumber starts by isolating the water lines and pressure testing sections to learn which side of the system leaks. Then acoustic equipment listens to the pipe through the concrete, and thermal imaging can show warm patches from hot-water lines.

When the location is narrowed, the plumber marks it on the floor. That lets the repair open a small area instead of a large one. Detection methods are described in more detail on the leak detection page.

Why slab leaks happen in the San Joaquin Valley

Slab leaks come from corroded pipe, pipe rubbing on concrete, poor installation or ground movement. The Corcoran area sits on the old Tulare Lake bed and is known for ground settling linked to groundwater pumping, so slabs and the pipes under them can be stressed over time.

Settling does not always crack a slab, but it can shift a pipe or a joint. If your home is older and has had other leaks, a plumber may recommend looking at the rest of the supply system as well.

Signs you may have a slab leak

  • The sound of water when everything is off.
  • Warm or damp spots on the floor.
  • A water bill higher than usual with no cause.
  • Cracks in walls or flooring that follow a line.

What to do before the plumber arrives

  • Shut off the main water valve and open a low faucet to drain the lines.
  • Turn off power to wet areas at the breaker only if you can reach it dry.
  • Move furniture and valuables out of the wet area.
  • Take photos of the damage before cleanup.
  • Stay clear of ceilings that are bulging or dripping.

Repair options: spot repair, reroute and replace

A spot repair breaks the slab above the leak, replaces the damaged section and patches the concrete. A reroute abandons the leaking line under the slab and runs a new line through walls or the attic. A replacement of the whole run is chosen when more than one section is failing.

Each option has a trade-off. Spot repair is the least disruptive but may not stop the next leak. Rerouting avoids the slab but changes the plumbing path. A full replacement leads into repiping and costs the most. The plumber should explain which fits.

Insurance, documentation and what to keep

Many homeowner policies cover water damage from a sudden leak and sometimes the cost of accessing the pipe, but they rarely cover the pipe itself. Policies differ, so call your insurer early.

Keep the detection findings, photos of the area and the repair invoice. A written description of where the leak was and how it was found helps with the claim.

Code and licensing for water supply work

Water supply piping is covered by the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). The UPC calls for a pressure regulator when supply pressure is above 80 psi, and it sets rules for approved pipe materials and shutoff valves.

Work on the service line between the meter and your house may need a permit, and the meter itself belongs to the City of Corcoran. Ask the city before you dig or cut into the line.

How to Detect a Leak in a Sprinkler System in Corcoran, CA

A quiet leak in the irrigation line can waste thousands of gallons. A meter test and a walk around the yard usually find it.

Written by the Corcoran Express Plumbing team · Published 2026

A sprinkler leak rarely announces itself. Water slips from a cracked pipe, a broken head or a stuck valve and sinks into the soil. You notice a soggy patch, a bill that looks high or sprinklers that spray weakly. In Corcoran, CA summers, where lawns and gardens need frequent watering, small leaks add up quickly.

This guide gives you a simple sequence to find a leak, the signs that point to each part of the system and the point at which a plumber's equipment becomes worth it. Always turn off the controller before digging.

Start with the water meter test

Turn off every fixture and appliance in the house, including the ice maker and the sprinklers. Find the water meter and look at the leak indicator, the small dial or triangle that spins when water moves. If it moves with everything off, water is going somewhere.

Now close the main shutoff to the house, or the valve that feeds the irrigation line only. If the meter stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the shutoff, which may be in the irrigation or the service line.

Where the leak is, based on what you see

Leaks near the house are more urgent than leaks at the far end of the yard.

Walk the yard with the system running

Run each zone one at a time and watch. Look for heads that spray weakly, spray in the wrong direction or leak at the base. Look for geysers, soggy ground and areas that stay wet after the system turns off. A zone with low pressure and a wet patch usually has a broken pipe nearby.

Also check for sunken or green spots in the lawn, which can mean a leak below the surface. Water follows the path of least resistance, so the wet spot may sit away from the actual break.

Common leak points

  • Broken or cracked sprinkler heads.
  • Loose connections at risers and nozzles.
  • Cracked lateral lines from roots or digging.
  • Stuck or leaking zone valves in the valve box.

Check the valve box

The valve box is the green lid in the lawn that covers the zone valves. Open it with a screwdriver, and look for standing water, a hissing sound or valves that stay damp. A valve that does not close fully will leak into the zone even when the controller is off.

Be careful of insects and spiders. Wear gloves and use a light. If the box is full of water, a bucket and a sponge can clear it so you can see the fittings.

Pressure and pipe tests

A pressure gauge on a hose bib can show the static pressure at the house. Too high a pressure can burst a pipe or blow heads. A pressure regulator, if one is installed, may have failed.

A plumber can pressure test an isolated line to find a leak you cannot see. For a line that runs under a driveway or patio, leak detection with acoustic equipment and a locator can mark the point without digging up the whole yard.

Fixing small leaks yourself

A broken head is a simple swap. Dig around it carefully, unscrew the old head, flush the line and thread on a new one. A cracked PVC pipe can be repaired with a coupling, but you must clean the ends and use the right primer and cement.

Before you dig, call 811 to have underground utilities marked. It is free and prevents damage to gas, water and power lines. If you find a leak near the water meter or main line, call a plumber.

Know what the leak costs you

A leak that loses one gallon a minute wastes about 1,440 gallons a day. Even a slow drip adds up over a billing cycle. If your bill has risen without a change in habits, a sprinkler leak is a likely cause.

A meter-side leak also creates a risk to the house, since saturated soil can undermine a slab. If the wet area is near the foundation, treat it as urgent and look at water service line repair.

When the leak is not in the sprinklers at all

Not every wet spot is an irrigation leak. A wet patch near the house may be a slab leak, a cracked sewer lateral or a failed service line. If the spot stays wet even when the sprinkler is off, the source is probably a pressurized line.

A plumber can tell the difference with a listening device or a pressure test. That saves digging in the wrong place.

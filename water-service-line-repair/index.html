A wet yard, low pressure or a meter that never stops can mean the buried line has failed. We locate and repair it.

Emergency water service line repair in Corcoran, CA is needed when the buried pipe from the meter to your house leaks or breaks. Signs include a soggy yard with no rain, a sudden pressure drop, a water bill that has jumped or the sound of water at the meter. The 24/7 line is open now, and a plumber can start the same day when schedules allow.

The service line is the pipe that carries city water from the meter or connection point to the house. The homeowner is generally responsible for the pipe on the house side, but exact responsibility depends on the city. Ask Corcoran Public Works where the city's part ends.

A broken service line: first steps

A broken line can wash out soil, flood a yard or undermine a driveway. If water is flowing and you cannot stop it, close the valve at the meter or at the house. If the valve will not close, call the city.

Signs of a service line leak

A wet, sunken or unusually green patch between the meter and the house, a pressure drop at every tap and a meter that moves with everything off all point to the service line. Sometimes you can hear water running in the ground near the meter.

A leak between the meter and the house is also a leak inside the property. If the meter is moving and the interior tests are clean, the plumber moves to the yard. The first steps are the same as in leak detection.

Low water pressure: service line or something else?

Low pressure at every fixture can come from a partly closed valve, a failed pressure regulator, a restricted service line or a city pressure issue. Pressure that is low at only one fixture is usually a local problem such as a clogged aerator.

A plumber can test pressure at a hose bib, check the regulator and compare the reading to normal home range. If the service line is the cause, the answer is repair or replacement. If the city pressure is low, the city is the contact.

Quick pressure checks

  • Compare hot and cold at one faucet.
  • Check whether the pressure fell at the same time everywhere.
  • Look for wet soil along the line.
  • Ask neighbors if they see the same drop.

What to do before the plumber arrives

  • Shut off the main water valve and open a low faucet to drain the lines.
  • Turn off power to wet areas at the breaker only if you can reach it dry.
  • Move furniture and valuables out of the wet area.
  • Take photos of the damage before cleanup.
  • Stay clear of ceilings that are bulging or dripping.

Repair, trenchless and replacement

A small leak at a fitting may be repaired with a short trench and a new coupling. A line that is old or leaking in several places is usually replaced. Some lines can be replaced with a trenchless method that pulls a new line through the old path with small pits at each end.

Material matters. Older lines may be galvanized steel, copper or older plastic, and newer lines are usually copper or approved plastic. The plumber will explain which type and which method suits the soil, the distance and any landscaping.

Soil, settling and shutoff access

Corcoran sits on the old Tulare Lake bed, and heavy clay soils shrink and swell with moisture and settle over time. A buried line can be pulled at joints or crushed by loads above it.

Know where your meter box and house main are. Keep the lid clear of dirt and plants so the valve can be reached in an emergency. If a line fails near the foundation, the problem may run into slab leak repair territory.

Code and licensing for water supply work

Water supply piping is covered by the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). The UPC calls for a pressure regulator when supply pressure is above 80 psi, and it sets rules for approved pipe materials and shutoff valves.

Work on the service line between the meter and your house may need a permit, and the meter itself belongs to the City of Corcoran. Ask the city before you dig or cut into the line.

Source type: state licensing board (Contractors State License Board license check), California Plumbing Code (UPC-based), city government website (City of Corcoran). Rules can change, so confirm current requirements.

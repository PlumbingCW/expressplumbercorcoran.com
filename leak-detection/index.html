Emergency Leak Detection in Corcoran, CA

A water bill that jumped or a meter that spins with everything off means a leak is hiding. We find it before the repair.

☎ Call (661) 338-1180

Emergency leak detection in Corcoran, CA is for the moment you find a wet ceiling, a warm spot on the floor, a soaked wall or a meter that keeps moving after every tap is closed. The 24/7 line is open, and a plumber can start with simple tests over the phone and decide whether a visit tonight is needed.

Leak detection means locating a leak without opening walls or digging at random. Acoustic listening devices, thermal imaging, pressure tests and tracer gas find the source so the repair cuts only where it must.

An active leak: what to do right now

Water that is running inside a wall or ceiling is an emergency, especially near electrical outlets or light fixtures. The first goal is to stop the water and protect people from shock.

Close the main water valve and keep clear of wet outlets. If water is dripping through a light fixture, turn off the breaker for that circuit from a dry spot.

Main valve off

Know where the shutoff is and turn it clockwise until it stops.

Breaker off

Turn off power to the wet area from the panel if you can reach it dry.

Catch and move

Use buckets and towels and lift furniture and electronics off the floor.

Take photos

Photograph the damage and the meter reading for the plumber and your insurer.

The meter-still-running test

The simplest leak test needs no tools. Turn off every fixture, appliance and irrigation line, then watch the meter's leak indicator or low-flow dial. If it keeps moving, water is escaping somewhere between the meter and the fixtures.

Meter practices differ by utility, so ask City of Corcoran Public Works how your account is metered and billed. A moving dial points to a leak on your side, and the plumber's job is to find where.

What to note before the call

  • The meter reading and the time you took it.
  • Which fixtures were off during the test.
  • Any wet spots, warm floor areas or musty smells.
  • A recent bill that was higher than usual.

High water bill and no visible leak

A bill that rises with no clear cause often traces to a running toilet, a leaking irrigation valve or a slow leak in a buried line. These do not show on the surface, so the detection visit starts with isolating sections of the system.

Once a leak is isolated, the plumber narrows it with sound, heat or gas. Then the repair opens a small area instead of a whole wall or yard.

What to do before the plumber arrives

  • Shut off the main water valve and open a low faucet to drain the lines.
  • Turn off power to wet areas at the breaker only if you can reach it dry.
  • Move furniture and valuables out of the wet area.
  • Take photos of the damage before cleanup.
  • Stay clear of ceilings that are bulging or dripping.

Ceiling, wall and under-house leaks

A stain on a ceiling can come from a supply line, a drain line or a roof. The plumber checks the plumbing first, then points you to other trades if the water is from outside.

In homes with raised foundations, leaks can drip into the crawlspace for months before showing inside. A look under the house often finds a damp patch, a rusted fitting or mold growth near a failed joint. Homes on slabs have a different pattern, covered on the slab leak repair page.

Irrigation, yard and meter-box leaks

Yards in the San Joaquin Valley run sprinklers for much of the year. A cracked valve, a broken head or a failed fitting in the meter box can waste water daily. Soggy lawn spots or sinking soil often mark the place.

If the leak is on the line from the meter to the house, the repair belongs on the water service line repair page. If it is in a sprinkler zone, the plumber can isolate it and recommend repair or replacement.

Code and licensing for water supply work

Water supply piping is covered by the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). The UPC calls for a pressure regulator when supply pressure is above 80 psi, and it sets rules for approved pipe materials and shutoff valves.

Work on the service line between the meter and your house may need a permit, and the meter itself belongs to the City of Corcoran. Ask the city before you dig or cut into the line.

Source type: state licensing board (Contractors State License Board license check), California Plumbing Code (UPC-based), city government website (City of Corcoran). Rules can change, so confirm current requirements.

DIY Water Filtration Systems for Corcoran, CA Homes

Many filters are easy to install yourself. The trick is choosing one that matches what you want to remove.

Written by the Corcoran Express Plumbing team · Published 2026

Filtering your water at home is a common project. Some systems need only a wrench and a free hour, and others involve cutting into pipes. The right choice starts with what you want to remove, not with the product that looks best online.

This guide compares the main DIY options, explains how to read the city's water quality report and shows when to hand the job to a plumber. It is written for Corcoran, CA homeowners and renters.

Start with the water report

Your city publishes an annual water quality report, also called a consumer confidence report. It lists the source, the tested contaminants and how results compare to the legal limits. Reading it tells you what you may want to filter.

A filter certified for the contaminant you care about is the only reliable choice. Look for third-party certification on the package, and check which standard it covers. A label that says only 'removes impurities' does not tell you anything.

Pitcher and faucet filters

Pitchers and faucet-mounted filters need no plumbing. They improve taste and remove chlorine and some metals, depending on the model. They are low cost and easy, but the flow is slow and cartridges need regular replacement.

They suit renters and small households. They do not protect the shower, the dishwasher or the water heater, since they treat only the water you pour or draw at one faucet.

Questions to ask before you buy

  • What does the certification say it removes?
  • How many gallons per cartridge?
  • Will it fit my faucet or cabinet?
  • What does a replacement cartridge cost?

Under-sink filters and reverse osmosis

Under-sink filters connect to the cold-water line and have a separate faucet or tee into the existing one. Reverse osmosis units add a membrane and a small storage tank. They remove a wider range of dissolved substances but waste some water and need space.

A handy owner can install a basic under-sink unit with a drill and a wrench. Drilling a countertop for a new faucet, or tying into a drain for RO, is where mistakes happen. Leaks under a sink can damage cabinets for weeks before anyone notices.

Whole-house sediment filters

A sediment filter in a housing near the main shutoff catches sand, rust and grit before they reach fixtures. It does not treat taste or dissolved chemicals. It is a good protector for water heaters, washers and faucets.

Installation means cutting the main line, adding unions and a bypass, and sealing the threads. A mistake can flood the house. Many owners hire a plumber for this one. The same visit can include the plumbing installation of shutoff valves and a pressure gauge.

Softeners are a separate question

A water softener removes calcium and magnesium, which cause scale. It does not filter contaminants. Whether you need one depends on your local hardness, and the answer is not the same in every town.

Read the report before you buy. See water softener and filtration service for what a plumber measures and recommends, including the honest case where a softener is not needed.

Maintenance is the real cost

A filter that is not changed becomes a place for bacteria to grow and it stops working. Write the install date on the housing and set a reminder. Keep spare cartridges on hand.

If the water has an odd smell or color, or you have an immune-compromised family member, test the water at a certified lab instead of relying on a filter alone.

Testing your water before you buy

A home test strip shows only a few basics. A certified lab test is the accurate way to measure hardness, nitrates, metals and microbes. California lists certified laboratories on a state website, and many labs sell mail-in kits for homeowners.

Test before and after installing a filter to see that it works. This matters most for private wells, where no utility checks the water for you.

Safety notes for DIY installs

Shut off the water and relieve pressure before you cut or loosen any line. Use the correct fittings for your pipe type, and use thread seal tape on threaded joints. Check every connection for drips after you turn the water back on, then check again the next day.

If you are not comfortable with pipes, hire a plumber for the connection and handle the cartridges yourself later. A small leak under the sink can ruin a cabinet over several weeks.

What DIY filters cannot do

A pitcher or cartridge filter cannot fix a water heater that is full of sediment, or a pipe that is corroding inside the wall. It treats only what passes through it. If the problem is in the plumbing, a filter hides the symptom without fixing the cause.

Corcoran, CA: what the city report shows

The City of Corcoran's 2021 water quality report lists average hardness of 52 ppm and average total dissolved solids of 240 ppm. Hardness at 52 ppm is soft water, so a softener is usually not needed for scale control in the city system. The report lists other tested substances too, and you should read it for the details before choosing a filter.

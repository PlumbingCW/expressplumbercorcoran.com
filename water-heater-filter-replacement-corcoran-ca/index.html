Water Heater Filter Replacement: A Corcoran, CA Homeowner Guide

Most tank heaters have no filter at all, but tankless units, inlet screens and whole-house cartridges do. Here is how to tell what you have.

Written by the Corcoran Express Plumbing team · Published 2026

People searching for a water heater filter usually mean one of three things: the small screen at the cold-water inlet, the filter on a tankless unit or a sediment filter installed on the line feeding the heater. A standard tank heater has no cartridge to swap, and that surprises many homeowners in Corcoran, CA.

This guide explains each type, how to find it, how often to service it and which jobs are safe for a handy owner. It also covers what to do when the real problem is sediment inside the tank rather than a clogged filter.

Which filters a water heater can have

A tank heater has a cold-water inlet and a hot-water outlet but no built-in filter. Some models have a plastic dip tube that sends cold water to the bottom of the tank, and that tube is not a filter. Any filter on a tank system sits on the pipe before the heater, not inside it.

Tankless heaters are different. Many have a small mesh screen at the cold-water inlet that catches grit before it reaches the heat exchanger. Some homes also have a whole-house sediment filter near the main shutoff, which protects every fixture and the heater together.

Signs a filter or screen is clogged

A clogged inlet screen shows up as weak hot-water flow while cold water at other taps stays strong. A tankless unit may show an error code, run a low flow or cycle on and off. A clogged whole-house cartridge lowers pressure at every tap.

Rust-colored or gritty water from the hot side only points to sediment in the tank, not a filter. That is a flushing or replacement question, so see water heater repair for what a plumber checks.

What to check first

  • Is the low flow on hot water only or on cold too?
  • Is there an error code on a tankless display?
  • Did the problem start after plumbing work or a water main repair?
  • Is there a filter housing near the main shutoff?

Cleaning a tankless inlet screen

Shut off the water and the gas or power. Close the isolation valves, open the service valves and let the trapped water drain into a bucket. Unscrew the filter screen from the cold-water inlet and rinse it under running water until the mesh is clear.

Reinstall the screen with its washer, open the valves slowly and purge air at a hot tap before restarting the unit. Many manufacturers ask owners to do this on a schedule, so read your manual. If you are not comfortable working near gas, schedule a tankless water heater service visit.

Replacing a whole-house sediment cartridge

The cartridge sits in a housing, usually a clear or blue canister. Shut off the water, relieve pressure with a nearby tap and unscrew the housing with the supplied wrench. Drop in the new cartridge, check the o-ring and tighten the housing by hand plus a small turn.

Cartridge life depends on how much sediment your water carries. A cartridge that is dark in a few weeks suggests a source of grit upstream, such as new construction on the street or a disturbed service line. Change it more often, and ask a plumber to check the line if the problem returns.

Flushing the tank instead

If sediment is inside a tank heater, a filter will not help. Draining a few gallons from the tank valve can remove loose sediment. An older heater with a thick layer on the bottom may crackle or rumble, and it may be near the end of its life.

When a tank is more than ten years old, repeated flushing is usually a short-term measure. A plumber can tell you whether the anode rod, the element or the tank is the issue, and whether water heater replacement makes more sense.

When to call a plumber

Call if you smell gas, see water leaking from the heater, find a corroded valve that will not turn or cannot restore flow after cleaning. Also call if the heater sits in an attic or tight closet where working with a wrench is unsafe.

A plumber can add isolation valves for easier future cleaning, install a sediment filter on the supply line and check the temperature and pressure relief valve. These small upgrades make later maintenance quick and reduce emergency calls.

Corcoran, CA: what the water report says about sediment

The City of Corcoran's 2021 water quality report lists hardness averaging 52 ppm and total dissolved solids averaging 240 ppm. Those are dissolved minerals, not the grit that clogs a screen, so scale on a tankless inlet is usually light here. Grit and rust more often come from old pipes inside the home or from work on the street.

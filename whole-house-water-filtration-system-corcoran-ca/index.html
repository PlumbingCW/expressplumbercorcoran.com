What Is a Whole House Water Filtration System? A Corcoran, CA Guide

A whole-house system treats water where it enters the home, so every tap and appliance gets the same water.

Written by the Corcoran Express Plumbing team · Published 2026

A whole-house water filtration system is a set of filters installed on the main water line, right after the shutoff. Every tap, shower and appliance receives treated water. Compare that with a pitcher or an under-sink unit, which treat only a single outlet.

This guide explains the stages, who benefits, what it costs to own and how it differs from a softener. It also describes the questions to ask a plumber before you buy in Corcoran, CA.

How a whole-house system works

Water enters the home through the main line and passes through one or more tanks or housings. Each stage does a different job. Sediment filters catch grit, carbon filters reduce chlorine and some chemicals and specialty media can target other substances.

The system needs a bypass so you can service it without shutting off the whole house. Pressure gauges before and after show when a stage is clogged.

The main stages

A sediment stage comes first, protecting the later stages from clogging. A carbon stage follows to improve taste and smell and to reduce chlorine. Some systems add a second stage for specific contaminants, an ultraviolet lamp for microbes or a softener.

Not every home needs every stage. More stages mean more cost, more maintenance and more pressure loss. Match the system to the water report and your goals.

What the stages usually do

  • Sediment: grit, sand and rust.
  • Carbon: chlorine, taste and odor.
  • Specialty media: targeted substances.
  • UV lamp: microbes, for private well users.

Who benefits most

Homes on private wells often need more treatment because no utility monitors the water. Homes with rusty old pipes may benefit from a sediment stage. People who want fewer chemicals in shower water may choose carbon.

Homes on a city system that already meets standards may need little. In that case a simple sediment filter is often enough, and an under-sink filter handles drinking water.

Installation and what to expect

A plumber cuts into the main line, adds shutoff valves, a bypass and unions and mounts the tanks on a wall or slab. The job takes several hours. Drain connections may be needed for backwashing systems.

Space matters. A typical system needs a few square feet near the main. Plan for access to change cartridges, and have a floor drain or a plan for overflow. See water softener and filtration for what we install.

Costs you should plan for

The purchase price is only the beginning. Cartridges, media replacement, electricity for UV and water used in backwashing are ongoing. Ask the plumber for a yearly maintenance estimate before you buy.

Be careful of sales pitches that promise to remove everything. No single filter does. A certification on the equipment is the best guide to what it removes.

Whole-house filter or softener?

A filter removes particles and some chemicals. A softener removes hardness minerals that cause scale. They solve different problems, and some homes need both, while many need neither.

Soft water already reduces scale, so a softener adds little. Read the hardness figure for your supply before you decide, and ask the plumber to explain the choice.

Pressure, flow and sizing

A whole-house system must pass all the water your home uses at peak times. An undersized unit restricts flow and causes pressure drops, especially when the shower and washer run together. The plumber sizes the system by the number of bathrooms, fixtures and the pipe size at the main.

Ask for the rated flow in gallons per minute and the pressure drop at that flow. A good system has little loss when it is clean.

Maintenance schedule to expect

Sediment cartridges often need changing every few months, and carbon stages last longer. UV lamps are usually replaced yearly. Backwashing systems regenerate on a timer and use water and sometimes salt.

Write the schedule on the housing and set reminders. A filter you forget becomes a source of problems instead of a fix.

Questions to ask a plumber

Ask what the system is certified to remove, what the yearly upkeep costs, how much water it wastes and what happens if you skip maintenance. Ask for the warranty terms in writing.

A trustworthy plumber will also tell you when you do not need a system, and point you to a simpler option.

When a simpler fix is enough

If your only concern is taste at the kitchen tap, an under-sink filter costs less and takes less space than a whole-house unit. If rusty water appears only after plumbing work, flushing the lines may be all you need.

Start with the problem you can describe, and let that decide the equipment.

Corcoran, CA: what to check before you buy

The City of Corcoran's 2021 water quality report lists hardness averaging 52 ppm and total dissolved solids averaging 240 ppm, with the source and tested substances listed in the report. Because the supply is soft, a softener is not a typical need in the city system. A filter decision should rest on the other results in the report and on what you notice at your taps.

If your property is on a private well outside the city, test the water at a certified lab, since no utility does it for you. Our Hanford plumbing page and service area page show where we dispatch. You can verify a plumber's license on the state board's website before work begins.
